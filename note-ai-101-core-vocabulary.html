AI 101 Core Vocabulary (Units 1–4)

🗂️ Linked Flashcards 🚀 Learn Mode

A comprehensive reference of foundational vocabulary, frameworks, and workflow concepts across AI 101.

Unit 1: Foundations (Project 1)

  • Inference Loop - The core operational loop of modern AI systems: Prompt → Model Inference → Output. Understanding this sequence prevents treating the tool like a traditional search engine or deterministic database.
  • Keep-or-Reject Rhythm - The active evaluation habit of reviewing every AI-generated suggestion or artifact individually. A creator evaluates each component and decides what to keep, what to modify, and what to reject outright, rather than passively accepting entire outputs.
  • Tool vs. Agent vs. System:
    • Tool: A single-purpose instrument operated directly by a human for an immediate task (e.g., a simple prompt, a spellchecker, a single script).
    • Agent: An autonomous or semi-autonomous entity given an intent, specific tools, operational boundaries, and context to take multi-step actions toward a goal.
    • System: An interconnected structure combining multiple tools, agents, files, memory, and human review gates into an integrated workflow.
  • Cognitive Offloading - The act of shifting cognitive tasks or mental effort onto external AI tools. Requires explicit tracking so creators know what routine work has been delegated versus what critical judgment and original thinking remain theirs.

Unit 2: The Design Process (Project 2)

  • Intent Statement - A written statement established before touching any AI tool, defining the project's purpose, intended user, scope, and non-negotiable boundaries.
  • Intent Gap - The discrepancy between what a creator originally intended to achieve and what the AI actually generated. Measuring this gap informs iterative prompt refinements.
  • Directed Pipeline - A structured workflow that chains two or more tools across multiple media types (such as text, image, code, or audio) in an intentional, sequential order with human review checkpoints.
  • Source Fidelity - The principle and verification loop ensuring that generated content, summaries, and flashcards adhere strictly to verbatim source material without introducing unverified outside facts or hallucinations.

Unit 3: Your Field (Project 3)

  • Disclosure Protocol - A transparent statement detailing exactly where and how AI contributed to a project, what prompts were used, and what human decisions and modifications took place.
  • Ethical Position Statement - A personal stance defining ethical boundaries regarding authorship, ownership, data consent, and agency when incorporating generative AI into creative practice.
  • Record of Resistance (RoR) - A dedicated log entry documenting every time a creator pushes back against, modifies, or rejects an AI recommendation to maintain intellectual control.
  • Audit Note - A stress-test analysis of an existing workflow or tool to identify structural friction points, failures, or over-reliance, followed by an actionable fix.
  • Defense Notes - Structured speaking notes prepared to defend an agent's architecture, context choices, failure modes, and boundaries in front of peers and instructors.

Unit 4: Orchestration (Project 4)

  • Orchestration - The coordination of multiple tools, prompts, context files, and operational boundaries toward a unified purpose without loss of human oversight.
  • Vibe Coding - An interactive, conversational method of writing functional software and games with AI assistance, guided by rigorous specifications and rapid verification rather than manual coding from scratch.
  • PRD (Product Requirements Document) - A concise, one-screen document outlining the project goal, primary user, up to three core features, user interaction flow, visual style, out-of-scope boundaries, and three concrete "Done when" verification criteria.
  • ADR (Architecture Decision Record) - A lightweight document recording a single technical or architectural choice, the problem it solves, the chosen solution, alternatives considered and rejected, and the reasoning behind the decision.
  • Build Sentence - A strict single-sentence formulation of a core mechanic: When [user] does [action], the build [responds with what].
  • Testing & Failure Log - A structured testing record capturing four identical test cases across Cycle 1 (baseline build) and Cycle 2 (revisions), documenting specific failures with screenshots before applying fixes.
  • Source Index - A comprehensive registry of every external reference, code snippet, asset, or library used in a build, including its source URL and explicit licensing or permission status.
